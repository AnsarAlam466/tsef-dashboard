# The Problem

## Finding student housing in Malaysia is broken

Every semester, thousands of international students arrive in Malaysia — from Saudi Arabia, Kenya, Bangladesh, Nigeria, China, and beyond — and face the same chaotic scramble for a room.

### What students deal with today

- **No trusted source.** Rooms are found through WhatsApp forwards, Facebook groups, and word of mouth. Listings are unverified, photos are outdated, and prices shift depending on who's asking.
- **Agent roulette.** Students message five agents to hear back from one. Each agent covers different buildings, holds different (often overlapping) inventory, and disappears mid-conversation.
- **Information asymmetry.** A student who just landed doesn't know that a master room at D'Latour should cost ~RM1,500/person, or that a 2-person unit can be split. They overpay or get placed badly.
- **Time pressure.** Semester start dates are fixed. Students routinely settle for whatever is left in the final week — at inflated prices.

### What landlords and agents deal with

- **No demand visibility.** Agents sit on empty units while students two blocks away can't find them.
- **Repetitive manual work.** The same photos, prices, and availability get retyped into hundreds of chats.
- **Lost deals.** Slow responses kill closings; there is no system tracking who asked for what.

### The gap

There is no verified, student-first marketplace connecting demand (students, by university, budget, and room type) with supply (agents and landlords, by building and availability) — in a market with hundreds of thousands of enrolled international students and continuous, semester-driven demand.

**Ten&See exists to close that gap.**
